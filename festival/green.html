Green Wilderwood

We measured our footprint in 2013, got a fright, and have spent every year since cutting it. It's down 91%. Here's exactly how, what it cost, and the bits we still haven't solved.

91%Footprint cut since 2013
100%Renewable electricity
11 yrsMeat & fish free
92%Of site waste diverted from landfill

The five big moves

Things that actually shifted the number

Plenty of green festival initiatives are lovely and change nothing. These are the five that did, ranked by how much carbon they took out.

Biggest single saving

1. Taking meat and fish off site

In 2016 we went fully vegetarian across every trader and every crew kitchen. One decision, more carbon saved than a decade of everything else put together. We lost four traders and about two hundred customers in the first year. Both came back.

Two thirds of our footprint

2. Making the coach cheaper than the car

Travel is the number nobody wants to look at. Rather than lecture people, we made the low-carbon ticket £40 cheaper and subsidised every seat. Car-free arrivals went from 24% to 61% in six years.

Since 2018

3. Killing the diesel generator

Site power is renewable grid electricity, waste-vegetable-oil generators where there's no connection, and solar on the info points and charging tent. No red diesel anywhere, including traders' pitches.

Since 2019

4. No single-use plastic, no exceptions

Deposit-return cups at every bar, free water refills everywhere, compostable serveware, and a rule for traders with no wriggle room in it. The awkward one was glitter; we now only allow the plant-based sort.

Ongoing

5. Leaving the land better

Compost loos feed the estate woodland, we've planted 9,000 trees with the landowner, and 40 acres of the park are now managed as wildflower meadow that gets cut after the festival, not before.

The honest bit

What we haven't fixed

Artist travel, especially international flights, is still 8% of our footprint and we book fewer long-haul acts than we'd like to admit is a choice. Camping gear abandoned on Monday is down but not solved. And we still can't get campervans below a number that embarrasses us.

“A festival is a town that exists for four days. If we can't run one well, what exactly are we telling everyone about running a country?”
Ro Bennett, co-founder

Measuring it

Published every year, audited every three

We count scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions including audience travel, which is the bit most events leave out because it makes the total look terrible. An independent assessor checks the method every three years and we publish the full report, footnotes and awkward findings included.

We don't buy offsets to claim a net-zero headline. We put the equivalent money into the coach subsidy, the tree planting and the estate's meadow restoration, and we call it what it is: spending, not cancelling.

Your part in it

Four things that make the biggest difference

  • Come by coach, train or bike
  • Take every last piece of your kit home
  • Bring a bottle and use the free water taps
  • Stay for the Monday litter-pick
