What on earth is it

A small village that appears once a year, then vanishes

Wilderwood is a 12,000-capacity festival in a Northamptonshire river valley — part music festival, part carnival, part daft communal experiment. We build a town out of scaffolding, bunting and borrowed brass instruments, live in it for four days, then take every last cable tie home with us.

There is no VIP area. There are no billboards for things you didn't ask to see. What there is: fourteen stages ranging from a 5,000-capacity meadow to a shed that fits eleven people, a woodland that behaves differently after dark, a properly good kids' field, and a fancy-dress parade that has genuinely stopped traffic on the A508.

Everyone who comes is part of the thing. Volunteer, perform, trade or crew — roughly a third of the site is built by people who arrived as punters.

Where you'll end up

Fourteen stages, six worlds

All areas & stages

Each corner of site is programmed by a different crew, with its own personality, its own bar and its own strong opinions about what time is bedtime.

Cap. 5,000

The Main Meadow

The big one. Headliners, afro-funk orchestras, and a Sunday-evening singalong you will not be able to explain to colleagues.

Until 4am

The Hollow

A sunken dancefloor in a natural amphitheatre. Disco, dub, house, and one hardware set a night that nobody remembers booking.

Bring a hat

Cantina Carnivale

Cumbia, brass, balkan, swing. A tin-roofed cantina with a dancefloor that has been described by the parish council as "enthusiastic".

After dark

Lantern Woods

Light installations, poetry in a treehouse, a puppet opera about the water table, and a piano somewhere in the middle of it.

Ages 0–12

Little Sprouts

Den building, mud kitchen, tiny discos at teatime, and a quiet tent for when it has all become a bit much (for children or adults).

Free classes

The Healing Orchard

Yoga at 8am, sound baths, a wood-fired sauna, herbalists, and the best place on site to sit down and say nothing for an hour.

Green Wilderwood

We'd rather be a decent festival than a big one

Fourteen years ago we worked out our footprint and got a bit of a fright. Since then we've cut it by 91%: the whole site runs on renewable electricity and waste vegetable oil, the bars are cup-deposit only, and we've been meat and fish free since 2016 — which saves more carbon than every travel measure we've ever tried, combined.

None of it is finished. The travel emissions of 12,000 people driving to a field are still the biggest number on our spreadsheet, which is why the Green Traveller ticket is £40 cheaper.
